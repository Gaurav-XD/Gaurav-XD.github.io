Business problem

Connect customer and product signals to decisions

Retail teams need a joined-up view of who is buying, which customers may become inactive, how customer value differs, and how product demand changes over time. This project demonstrates an analytical workflow for those questions and explores how outputs might inform retention and inventory decisions.

Churn

Identify customers with no purchase in a defined future observation window.

Customer value

Explore segmentation, retention patterns, and a transparent CLV revenue proxy.

Product demand

Build product-month demand features and compare forecasting approaches.

Business impact

Translate model outputs into explicit scenarios, not claims of realized ROI.

End-to-end data science workflow

From a defined question to decision support

1. Business framing & data preparation

Define synthetic retail data contracts, generate reproducible inputs, validate data quality, and explore customer and transaction patterns.

2. Point-in-time feature engineering

Build customer behavior features and lagged, shifted rolling demand features using only information available at each prediction cutoff.

3. Modeling & validation

Compare classical models, tune using training-only procedures, and use out-of-time churn evaluation and chronological demand validation.

4. Business impact & local prototypes

Quantify assumption-based scenarios and expose existing model artifacts through local API and dashboard prototypes.

Customer analytics

Descriptive customer value and predictive risk

RFM segmentation

Deterministic recency, frequency, and monetary scoring creates interpretable customer groups for exploration.

Cohort retention

First-observed-purchase cohorts track customer and revenue retention across matured monthly periods.

CLV proxy

A one-year revenue run-rate proxy annualizes observed revenue; it is not discounted lifetime profit or a validated probabilistic CLV model.

Churn prediction

Predictors are rebuilt at customer snapshots; churn is defined as no purchase in the following 90 calendar days.

Churn modeling

Compare interpretable and ensemble baselines

Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting were evaluated. Training-only three-fold stratified, unshuffled cross-validation selected the tuned Random Forest by average precision. Evaluation uses disjoint customer groups at earlier and later feature snapshots; it is an out-of-time holdout design, not chronological inner cross-validation.

Tuning and selected model

Randomized search considered six configurations per model. The selected Random Forest was refit on the training data before test evaluation.

Demand forecasting

Chronological product-month forecasts

A seasonal-naive lag-12 baseline and classical ML models were compared. Features include prior-month demand lags and rolling means and standard deviations calculated from shifted history, plus known calendar features. Validation cutoffs are shared across products; tuning uses training-period expanding-calendar folds only.

Walk-forward evaluation refits before each validation month and assumes prior actual demand is available. It is one-step rolling evaluation, not fixed-origin multi-step forecasting. Only two training CV folds were available, and validation results were reused during development.
Business impact & ROI

Scenario estimates are not business results

The churn scenario scores only the 965-customer held-out subset. At a 0.5 risk threshold, 456 are marked high risk. The calculations use historical revenue proxies and assumed intervention effectiveness; probabilities are not calibrated and no intervention was delivered.

Assumptions behind the estimate

  • 10% relative churn reduction among customers above the risk threshold.
  • 30% contribution margin and 5 source-currency units per contact.
  • ROI = (protected revenue × margin − contact cost) / contact cost.
  • Forecast inventory exposure uses separate assumed unit revenue, lost-sale share, and holding cost.
These are reproducible scenario calculations on synthetic data, not measured revenue protection, realized ROI, savings, or causal impact. Currency units are unspecified; excluded costs and actual intervention response could change the result.
Causal / uplift analysis

A placebo diagnostic, not causal evidence

Treatment assignment in the current workflow is hypothetical and retrospective; no retention intervention was delivered. The observed differences and ranked uplift-style scores do not establish causal treatment effects or show which customers would benefit.

The next valid step would be a prospective randomized experiment with verified treatment delivery, prespecified outcomes, and an evaluation plan established before results are observed.

Engineering & deployment

Reusable pipelines with local execution evidence

PySpark

DataFrame joins and aggregations reproduced the pandas customer feature contract in local Spark 3.5.9 and reconciled 5,000 customers across 23 columns.

Databricks-compatible example

A notebook-source entry point reuses the Spark pipeline. It is compatibility evidence only; Databricks execution has not been performed.

MLflow

Two local file-based experiments contain nine runs. Existing results were tracked retrospectively; tracking is not integrated into a model registry or production training service.

FastAPI & Streamlit

Local inference API and read-only dashboard prototypes were tested locally. They are not deployed services and have no production access controls or monitoring.

Validation & limitations

What the evidence does—and does not—show

Verified within the project

  • Automated project test suite passed locally.
  • PySpark customer outputs reconciled against the pandas reference with documented tolerances.
  • Chronological one-step forecasting validation and walk-forward outputs are recorded.
  • FastAPI and Streamlit prototypes were exercised locally.

Important limitations

  • All source datasets are synthetic; no commercial outcomes are measured.
  • Transaction coverage is inferred from observed dates, not independently proven complete.
  • Churn holdout and earlier forecasting test results have been inspected; fresh independent evaluation is needed.
  • PostgreSQL examples were not executed; Databricks and cloud execution were not performed.
  • Docker production deployment, monitoring, model governance, and causal treatment effects are not demonstrated.
